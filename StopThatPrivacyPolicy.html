Privacy Policy

For the Stop That app by Codewati

Effective: 3 October 2026 Package: com.codewati.stopthat

1. Overview

Stop That by Codewati is an on-device digital wellbeing and screen-time control app. You can select apps to monitor, set individual or shared category limits, configure weekly Study Time and Bed Time schedules, use Parent Mode with PIN protection, and review usage insights and focus goals.

The current Android app works without an account, developer-operated backend, advertising SDK, analytics SDK, or automatic cloud sync. Codewati does not automatically receive your usage history or settings.

2. Information Processed on Your Device

To provide these features, Stop That accesses or stores the following information locally:

  • installed launcher-app names, package identifiers, icons, and available app categories for selection, grouping, and identifying protected apps;
  • package-level usage events and timestamps supplied by Android Usage Access;
  • selected-app names and identifiers, session start/end times, durations, daily screen time, and app-open counts;
  • warning markers and restriction-event records, including the app, date, time, and type of restriction;
  • selected apps, per-app and shared category limits, and your Always Allowed exceptions;
  • Study Time and Bed Time start/end times, repeat days, and whether the relevant modes or schedules are enabled;
  • Get Me In extra-time and rest settings, the daily allowance and usage count, and recovery/rest state;
  • social-media, individual-app, and study-time goal settings and the date from which goals are evaluated;
  • consent, pause/reminder state, Normal/Parent/Student mode, appearance preferences, and widget state;
  • Parent Mode PIN verification data, as explained in section 6.

Android Usage Access can expose activity events for apps beyond your selection. Stop That processes these events locally to detect when a selected app is entered or left and saves the selected-app sessions used by its current monitoring features. Monitoring can continue while Stop That is not on screen, when enabled and permitted by Android.

Seven-day and 30-day insights, averages, top apps, period comparisons, distracting-hour summaries, goal streaks, and upcoming-restriction information are calculated on the device from local records and settings. These reporting periods do not mean older records are automatically deleted.

Older installations may retain a separate local automatic-history database. Its retained history is not merged with selected-app monitoring totals. The older background reconciliation and alert flow is retired in the current build.

3. Permissions and Android Special Access

Usage Access: reads package-level usage events to detect selected-app activity and calculate usage. Access is enabled by you through Android Settings following the in-app disclosure.

Display over other apps: shows reminders, app/category-limit screens, Study Time and Bed Time restrictions, Parent PIN prompts, Get Me In choices, and eye-rest screens over selected apps.

Foreground service: keeps selected-app monitoring running while enabled. The service creates a foreground-service notification; its visibility and Android's active-app indicators depend on system settings. The current app does not request the POST_NOTIFICATIONS runtime permission.

Boot and app-update handling: attempts to restart monitoring after reboot or an app update if consent, setup, selected apps, and the necessary permissions remain available and monitoring is not paused. Android or manufacturer restrictions can prevent or delay restart.

Installed-app visibility: queries launcher apps for the app picker and app categories. Recognized sensitive and essential apps are protected by local rules. Recognition is not exhaustive. Your Always Allowed exceptions bypass blocking; selected apps marked Always Allowed can still have their usage recorded.

System document picker: used only when you choose to export a CSV or PDF report. The app writes the report to the document location you select without requesting broad access to your files.

Device Policy support: applies only to specially provisioned managed devices, as described in section 9. The Protection Health screen reads permission, monitoring-service, boot-configuration, and Device Owner status locally. It does not transmit a health report to Codewati.

Stop That does not use Android Accessibility Service. It does not read other apps' chats, screen text, passwords, OTPs, payment details, banking content, screenshots, keyboard input, or touch input. It does not access your camera, microphone, contacts, or precise location. It does process the settings and PIN you enter directly into Stop That.

4. Network Use and Sharing

The current Android manifest does not declare the INTERNET permission. Stop That does not automatically upload usage sessions, installed-app selections, limits, schedules, goals, restriction records, or PIN verification data to Codewati. It does not sell these records or automatically share them with advertisers, analytics providers, or data brokers.

External privacy-policy, developer, social, or email links may open your browser or another app. Those services handle information under their own privacy policies. No usage report is automatically attached to a support email. User-initiated report exports are explained below.

5. CSV and PDF Report Exports

In Insights, you can choose to export the selected seven-day or 30-day report. Reports contain dates, app names, open counts, screen-time totals, current configured app limits, and recorded restriction-event counts. CSV reports also include app package identifiers. PIN verification data is not included.

The displayed limits are your current settings, not historical snapshots of every previous limit. Restriction-event counts cover events recorded by the version that supports this feature; older daily warning markers are not treated as a complete record of past blocks.

Reports are generated on your device and saved only after you select a destination in Android's document picker. If you choose a cloud-backed document provider, that provider may upload or sync the file under its own policies, even though Stop That itself has no INTERNET permission. Codewati does not automatically receive exported reports.

Exported files contain usage information and may be readable by anyone with access to the selected location or anyone you share them with. They are separate from the app's private storage. Clearing Stop That's storage or uninstalling it does not delete exported files or copies held by recipients or document providers; manage those copies separately through the relevant provider or app.

6. Local Storage, PIN Security, and Backup

Usage sessions, warning markers, and restriction events are stored in a private local database. Selections, schedules, limits, goals, consent, modes, and recovery settings are stored in private app preferences. Device security also depends on Android, your screen lock, and who can access your device.

Parent PINs are not stored as plain text. Stop That stores a salted PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 derived value, its random salt, PIN length, and format version to verify the PIN locally. The PIN protects designated in-app actions; it does not encrypt exported reports or act as a separate lock for all usage history.

The app disables Android app backup and configures its private data to be excluded from Android cloud backup and device-transfer backup. This does not control backup or syncing of reports you export to other locations.

7. Retention, Deletion, and Your Controls

Selected-app sessions, restriction records, and settings remain in app storage until you clear the app's storage or uninstall it; there is no automatic deletion tied to the seven-day or 30-day Insights view. Older upgrade data may also remain in private storage. Pausing monitoring, removing an app from your selection, or withdrawing consent does not erase previously saved history.

Depending on the active mode, you can:

  • pause or resume monitoring and reminders;
  • add or remove selected apps, edit limits, and manage Always Allowed exceptions;
  • change weekly schedules, Get Me In settings, and focus goals;
  • switch modes, subject to Parent PIN checks where applicable;
  • choose “Withdraw consent & stop reminders” in Settings to stop monitoring;
  • revoke Usage Access or Display over other apps through Android Settings;
  • remove the home-screen widget or delete exported report files separately.

Withdrawing in-app consent does not itself change Android's permission switches. On a normal installation, delete private app data through Android Settings > Apps > Stop That > Storage (or Storage & cache) > Clear storage, or uninstall the app. This does not erase usage records maintained separately by Android, other apps, or exported reports. Codewati cannot remotely retrieve or erase local records that have not been sent to us, and there is no Stop That account to delete.

On a managed Device Owner installation, Android may restrict uninstall or user-control actions while Parent Mode protection is active. The authorized parent or device administrator may need to remove that protection first.

8. Parent Mode, Schedules, and Focus Features

Parent Mode: a 4–6 digit PIN protects designated changes, including limits, category settings, Get Me In settings, schedules, goals, and Always Allowed exceptions. PIN verification is also used for applicable post-limit Get Me In requests. Processing and verification take place on the device.

Student Mode and Bed Time: selected apps can be blocked during configured weekly study or sleep periods. Student Mode also applies stricter daily app limits. Shared category limits combine usage from selected, non-exempt apps in that category. Always Allowed exceptions take priority over restrictions.

Get Me In: Normal/Parent flows can offer configurable additional usage followed by a rest period, subject to a daily maximum shared across selected apps. Settings and daily counters remain local. This option does not override every restriction, such as an active category, study, or bedtime block.

Goals and streaks: local goal settings are compared with recorded completed-day usage. These summaries are not sent to a parent, school, employer, or Codewati automatically. Android's event availability, permissions, and service interruptions can affect recorded usage and enforcement; the app is not a guarantee against every way of accessing another app.

9. Optional Device Owner Protection

If Android has specially provisioned Stop That as the Device Owner on a managed device, Parent Mode can ask Android to block uninstall of Stop That and restrict supported user-control actions for its package. These actions use Android Device Policy locally and do not create remote monitoring or a cloud parent account.

A normal installation, an in-app Parent PIN, and managed Device Owner protection are different. Stop That does not silently become a Device Owner. Android provisioning is required, and the Protection Health screen indicates whether this managed-device capability is available.

10. Home-Screen Widget and On-Device Visibility

The optional widget displays selected-app names, icons, open counts, screen-time summaries, and active mode through your launcher. The app's history, Insights, and reminder overlays can also show usage information to someone viewing your screen. Consider who can access your device and exported reports. You can remove the widget at any time.

11. Children and Family Use

A parent or guardian may configure Parent Mode, Student Mode, schedules, or limits for family use. The current build has no remote parent dashboard, child account, cloud monitoring, or server-side child profile. The same local data processing described in this policy applies when the device is used by a child.

Parents or guardians should explain the monitoring to the device user and manage setup, permissions, the Parent PIN, and any consent required for their use. Reports are shared outside the device only when someone chooses to export them to an external provider or share them using another service.
